I spent 3 years working with embedded Linux.
Turns out I was using maybe 10% of what the CLI can do.

A month into the KubeCraft Career Accelerator, I realized how lost I was on Linux basics.

So I made a choice that felt counterproductive at first:

I became a beginner again.

Not in a "watch YouTube tutorials" way.

In a "relearn how to type, switch to vim, live in the terminal" way.

Right now I type like my grandmother because I'm learning proper touch typing from scratch.

It's painful. It's slow. And it's one of the best decisions I've made.

The goal?

→ Be able to manage any Linux server, anywhere, with just a shell and bash.
→ Learn anything new using only a terminal.
→ Stop relying on tools that abstract away fundamentals.

Two months in — I feel like I've learned more than in the previous ten months combined.

Going back to zero isn't failure.

It's the fastest way forward.
